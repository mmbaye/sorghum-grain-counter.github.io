Requirements

📷 Image format

Accepted formats: JPEG / PNG
Maximum file size: 200 MB
Best results: image taken on a uniform background (black, white, or contrasting color).

💻 Technical

The application runs locally via R Shiny.
Python environment yolo (Conda) must be active with Ultralytics and OpenCV installed.

✅
Best image quality: Take photos in good natural light, hold the panicle flat against a dark background, and ensure all grains are visible without excessive overlap.

Upload an Image

📂

Click "Browse…" in the sidebar

In the Upload Image panel on the left, click the Browse… button and select your sorghum photo from your computer.

🖼️

Preview appears automatically

Once the upload is complete (green bar: "Upload complete"), a small thumbnail of your image appears in the sidebar for confirmation.

👁️

View the original image

Detection Settings

ParameterDefaultRangeEffect
Confidence Threshold 0.25 0.05 – 0.95 Minimum confidence for a detection to be counted. Lower = more detections (more false positives possible). Higher = fewer, more certain detections.
Max Detections 10 000 100 – 50 000 Hard cap on the number of bounding boxes returned. Increase only if your panicle is expected to have more than 10 000 grains.
⚠️
Threshold calibration: For dense panicles photographed close up, start with 0.25. If too many false positives appear (boxes on background), raise to 0.40–0.50. If grains are missed, lower toward 0.10.
4

Mass Conversion

The app converts the grain count into an estimated total mass using a simple linear formula:

Estimated Mass (g) = Slope × Grain Count Default slope = 0.028 g/grain — adjust for your sorghum variety

To calibrate the slope for your specific variety: weigh a known number of grains (e.g. 100 grains → 2.8 g → slope = 0.028). Enter your measured slope in the Slope (g per grain) field.

5

Run Detection

▶️

Click "Count Grains"

Press the large green Count Grains button at the bottom of the sidebar. The app calls the Python inference script in the background.

⏳

Wait for inference

Processing time depends on image size and number of grains. Typically 5–30 seconds. The app will switch to the Results tab automatically when done.

✅

Confirm success

A green message — "✓ N grains detected." — appears below the button in the sidebar once inference is complete.

🖼️ Annotated Image

The annotated image shows the original photo with blue bounding boxes drawn around each detected grain. Each box corresponds to one counted grain. Dense areas may show overlapping boxes — this is normal for tightly packed panicles.

📊 Confidence Distribution

The histogram shows how confident the model is for each detection. The red dashed line marks the mean confidence. A distribution skewed toward high values (0.8–1.0) indicates a high-quality image with clearly visible grains. A flatter distribution may indicate challenging lighting or image quality.

FAQ & Troubleshooting

The app says "Inference failed" — what do I do?

Check that: (1) the Python environment yolo is correctly set up with Ultralytics and OpenCV installed; (2) the path to WEIGHTS_PATH in app.R points to a valid .pt file; (3) the uploaded image is a valid JPEG or PNG. The error details are shown in the notification.

The grain count seems too high — many false positives

Increase the Confidence Threshold slider (e.g. from 0.25 to 0.45 or 0.60). This discards lower-confidence detections. You can also improve image quality: use a uniform background and ensure good lighting.

Some grains are not detected (missed detections)

Lower the Confidence Threshold (e.g. to 0.10–0.15). Also check that grains are not heavily overlapping or out of focus. If the model was not trained on your specific grain type, results may be limited.

The estimated mass seems wrong

Calibrate the Slope (g per grain) for your specific variety. Weigh a sample of 50–100 grains on a precision scale, divide the total mass by the number of grains, and enter this value as the slope.

Can I process multiple images in one session?

Yes — simply upload a new image and click Count Grains again. Each run overwrites the previous result. To compare results, note the counts manually or export the confidence data from the Detection Detail tab.

What image resolution gives the best results?

At least 1024 × 1024 px is recommended. Very high resolutions (> 4000 px) may slow inference but generally improve accuracy on dense panicles. Avoid blurry or heavily compressed images.
